## Deep Digests

### How Virus-like 'Jumping Genes' Became Our Partners in Evolution
[quantamagazine.org](https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-virus-like-jumping-genes-became-our-partners-in-evolution-20260921/)

Problem: Transposons ("jumping genes") make up nearly half the human genome, long dismissed as parasitic junk DNA.
Approach: Researchers traced transposon insertions across species, from McClintock's corn work to peppered-moth genomes, mapping when these elements reshaped genomes.
Result: One transposon in a peppered moth gene drove its 1819 shift to dark wings; transposons also built animal eyes, immune systems, and mammalian placentas, jumping between species nearly 1,000 times.
Why it matters: Transposons look less like invaders and more like coevolving partners hosts actively regulate, driving both rapid adaptation and genome regulation itself.
Glossary: Transposon - a DNA sequence that can move within a genome. Epigenetic control - regulating gene activity without changing DNA. Horizontal transfer - genes moving between species.

### Circular mRNA Helps Cell Factories Make Up to Six Times as Much Protein
[phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-circular-mrna-cell-factories-protein.html)

Problem: Bacterial mRNA lacks the caps and tails protecting it elsewhere, so it degrades in minutes, capping protein output.
Approach: Seoul National University built CRESEnT: self-splicing intron fragments arranged in reverse order so an mRNA's own ends join into a closed ring, no external enzymes needed.
Result: Circularized mRNA boosted protein output 5.95x and mRNA levels 3.95x versus linear controls, with 1.5-2.7x gains on compounds like lycopene, across three bacterial species.
Why it matters: Circular RNA resists degradation, having no open ends for RNases to grab — a drop-in yield boost for existing microbial drug and biofuel lines.
Glossary: mRNA - carries a gene's instructions to a cell's protein-making machinery. Self-splicing intron - an RNA segment that cuts and rejoins itself. RNase - an enzyme that degrades RNA.
