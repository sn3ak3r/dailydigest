### Ransomware Incidents in Japan in the First Half of 2026
[blog.talosintelligence.com](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/ransomware-incidents-in-japan-in-the-first-half-of-2026/)

Problem: Japan saw 90 ransomware incidents in H1 2026, up 4.7%, with small and mid-sized firms making up most victims.
Approach: Talos researchers picked apart open directories, leaked bash histories, and Python scripts left behind by two groups: The Gentlemen and Qilin.
Result: The Gentlemen run a disciplined six-phase playbook: VPN access, Nmap/Masscan recon, a GLPI exploit, credential theft via Responder/Impacket, then AD lateral movement and cloud exfiltration, with Russian-language artifacts in their tools. Qilin's attack scripts show signs of being AI-generated: uniform doc-style headers and a stray "llm_chatbot" folder in its command history.
Why it matters: Ransomware crews are professionalizing their tradecraft and now outsourcing script-writing to AI, shrinking the skill and time needed to build working attack tooling.
Glossary: Lateral movement - moving between compromised systems inside a network. Exfiltration - stealing data out of a network. Active Directory - Windows' directory service for accounts and permissions. RaaS - ransomware sold as a subscription service to affiliates.

### Where Does the Quantum World End and Ours Begin?
[quantamagazine.org](https://www.quantamagazine.org/where-does-the-quantum-world-end-and-ours-begin-20260917/)

Problem: Quantum objects behave like fuzzy waves that interfere with themselves, yet everyday objects have sharp, definite positions; physicists still lack a settled account of exactly where that switch happens.
Approach: The piece surveys decoherence theory (environment scrambling quantum information), the "decoherent histories" reformulation that drops the need for an outside observer, and Leggett-Garg-style experiments testing whether a system had a definite property before measurement, pushed up to virus-sized molecules.
Result: Every test so far still matches quantum mechanics exactly: interference survives even in large molecules, and it's the environment's bombardment, not a new law, that buries quantum effects at macroscopic scale.
Why it matters: Pinning down this boundary experimentally shapes how far quantum computers can scale and what "measurement" really means.
Glossary: Decoherence - environment destroying a quantum system's ability to interfere with itself. Superposition - existing in multiple states at once. Entanglement - distant particles staying correlated. Leggett-Garg test - experiment checking if a system had a definite state before measurement.
