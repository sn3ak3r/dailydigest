## Deep Digest: YouTuber gets classic Tomb Raider running on a chip that sips a mere 1 W of power

**Problem:** Can a full 3D-era game run on hardware never meant for gaming — a cheap embedded chip built for cameras and IoT gadgets?

**Approach in plain words:** Hobbyist Alejandro Villegas Alonso ported OpenLara, an open-source rebuild of Tomb Raider's engine, to the ESP32-P4 dev board ($30, meant for embedded devices). Rendering happens in software at low resolution, then scaled up to a touchscreen by a hardware pixel accelerator, with USB keyboard input and an audio codec for sound.

**Result:** Tomb Raider runs smoothly on a 1 W, dual-core RISC-V microcontroller; the attached LCD screen actually draws more power than the chip itself.

**Why it matters:** Open-source engine reimplementations plus commodity embedded silicon now make PC-class retro 3D gaming possible on hardware built for security cameras.

**Jargon glossary:** SoC (system-on-chip), RISC-V (open CPU instruction set), OpenLara (open-source Tomb Raider engine clone), Pixel Processing Accelerator (hardware image up-scaler).

## Deep Digest: Black Holes or Black Hole Stars? Astronomers Spar Over Webb's 'Little Red Dots.'

**Problem:** JWST keeps finding tiny, blazingly bright "little red dots" from the early universe that don't behave like ordinary supermassive black holes — no flickering, no X-rays.

**Approach in plain words:** Astronomers split the dots' light into detailed spectra, hunting for two signatures: broadened hydrogen colors (gas swirling fast around a black hole) and a sharp color jump called a Balmer break, normally seen in stars.

**Result:** Two dots show both signatures together, leading one camp to propose "black hole stars" — a black hole buried in a giant hydrogen shell, acting like a star's fusion core. Rivals argue ordinary black holes seen edge-on through dusty gas explain it too.

**Why it matters:** Confirming black hole stars would reveal a brand-new stage in how the universe's earliest giant black holes formed.

**Jargon glossary:** little red dots, Balmer break, broad line, quasi-star.
