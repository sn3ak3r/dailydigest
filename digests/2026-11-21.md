### Network Researchers Nail Down Theory of 'Perfect Resilience'

**Problem:** When network links fail, can routers still deliver every packet, using only local information and no knowledge of where the packet has been?

**Approach in plain words:** Bentert and Schmid (TU Berlin) looked for small "forbidden" structures that make this impossible. They kept trying to prove that networks without the known ones always work. Each time a proof failed, they found another forbidden structure.

**Result:** There are exactly four forbidden structures. Networks that contain none of them can always route around failures, using a fast "always turn right" maze-style rule. Networks that contain any of them can't. The paper was presented at STOC 2026.

**Why it matters:** It settles a question open since 2012. Surprisingly, adding links can make perfect resilience impossible.

**Jargon:** *Graph minor*: a network simplified by deleting or merging parts. *Forbidden minor*: a structure whose presence rules out a property. *Local routing*: forwarding decisions made per router. *STOC*: a top theory conference.
