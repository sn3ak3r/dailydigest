## Deep Digests

### Enormous Fluid Simulation on Flip Dots

**Problem:** Surplus flip-dot panels are painful to source and drive, but mitxela wanted a giant interactive "water" display for EMF 2026.

**Approach:** Built custom driver boards for each dot, ran a real-time fluid simulation across the grid, and added a joystick plus an accelerometer so tilting or shaking the cabinet stirs the simulated liquid.

**Result:** A wall of clacking dots that ripples and sloshes convincingly, looks almost 3D from an angle, and reacts physically when nudged.

**Why it matters:** Shows how much custom electronics and software it takes to make old electromechanical hardware feel alive, not just nostalgic.

**Jargon glossary:** Flip-dot display – pixel flipped by a magnet; Fluid simulation – physics model of liquid motion; Accelerometer – motion/tilt sensor.

### Reverse Engineering Apple's Mikey Chip

**Problem:** iPod Classic headsets use an undocumented "Mikey" chip for mic power and buttons; nobody had cracked its protocol in 20 years.

**Approach:** Hemant found one old archived blog post, traced the chip to an I2C bus, found each button is a resistive load causing a voltage drop, then built debug firmware into Rockbox to capture the handshake "chirp."

**Result:** Decoded button detection and the chirp handshake, then submitted the work to Rockbox for official support.

**Why it matters:** Restores wired-headset controls on vintage iPods and shows patient signal analysis can crack silicon with no documentation.

**Jargon glossary:** I2C bus – two-wire chip protocol; Resistive load – button read as resistance; Rockbox – open-source iPod firmware; Chirp – the handshake signal.
