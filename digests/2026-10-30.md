### Inside KeyDrift: Analyzing historical UAT-11473 activity on Cisco SD-WAN
https://blog.talosintelligence.com/uat-11473-keydrift/

**Problem:** Cisco SD-WAN controllers were compromised via CVE-2026-20127 (auth bypass) by UAT-11473, a likely Volt Typhoon-linked actor targeting space, defense and policy orgs. **Approach:** Custom KeyDrift malware persists via modified runit service scripts, uses ptrace to inject PolyFlame (a memory-resident backdoor/relay) into vdaemon processes, and LittleFilch hooks SSL_read to grab data after decryption. **Result:** It harvests peer IPs and a 32-byte key field (likely AES-256 IPsec key material), RSA-encrypts it and sends it out over UDP in DTLS-style records. **Why it matters:** It runs inside trusted controller software, so C2 hides in legitimate control-plane traffic and stolen keys could unlock the data plane. **Glossary:** runit (service supervisor); DTLS (TLS for UDP); OMP (Cisco SD-WAN control protocol); ptrace (Linux process-control call used for injection).
