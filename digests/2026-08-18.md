## Deep Digest 1: Certighost — CA Privilege Escalation to Full Domain Compromise

**Problem:** Active Directory Certificate Services can silently issue a certificate that lets an attacker impersonate a Domain Controller.

**Approach, in plain words:** When an Enterprise CA can't find a target machine locally, it follows a routing hint (the "cdc" parameter) the requester supplies — and never checks that the hint actually points to a real Domain Controller. An attacker points it at a rogue machine they control, which feeds the CA forged identity data.

**Result:** A standard low-privilege domain user got a certificate bound to a DC's identity, used it via PKINIT to obtain a Kerberos ticket as that DC, then ran DCSync to steal the krbtgt hash — full domain takeover from ordinary user access.

**Why it matters:** Trust infrastructure (the CA) had no verification step of its own, showing how default settings and implicit trust chains, not just bugs, create critical paths to compromise.

**Jargon glossary:**
- **PKINIT** — Kerberos extension enabling certificate-based authentication.
- **DCSync** — a directory-replication trick used to extract password hashes.
- **krbtgt** — the account whose key signs all Kerberos tickets in a domain.
- **Enterprise CA** — an AD-integrated certificate authority trusted domain-wide.

## Deep Digest 2: Theory of Fluids Enters the 21st Century

**Problem:** The 1800s Navier-Stokes equations model fluids as smooth continuums, ignoring that fluids are actually made of discrete jostling molecules.

**Approach, in plain words:** Physicists applied "effective field theory" — a framework for figuring out which physical effects matter at which scale — to fluids, by cataloging their symmetries. Fluids, like the expanding universe, break a certain space-time symmetry, and they also have unlimited freedom to swap interior parcels without cost, unlike solids. Combining these symmetries with a time-reversal rule borrowed from black hole physics let researchers rebuild Navier-Stokes from scratch.

**Result:** The derivation surfaced small correction terms in the classical equations that capture molecular-scale motion, previously ignored, plus new equations for exotic matter like fractons.

**Why it matters:** A fluid can now be defined purely by its symmetries rather than its chemistry — explaining why wildly different substances behave alike, and predicting new fluid-like phases of matter.

**Jargon glossary:**
- **Symmetry** — a transformation that leaves a system's physics unchanged.
- **Effective field theory** — a method for isolating which terms matter at a given scale.
- **Viscosity** — a fluid's intrinsic resistance to flow.
- **Fracton** — an exotic particle that can only move in coordinated groups.
