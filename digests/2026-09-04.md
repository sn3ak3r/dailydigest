## Deep Digests

### A Split Keyboard Designed for Human Hands
**Problem:** Standard keyboards still copy the 19th-century typewriter layout — flat rows, a shared spacebar hogging both thumbs — which doesn't match how hands and fingers actually move.
**Approach:** Paul iterated roughly 50 times on a split, columnar keyboard where each finger's column is angled and spaced for that finger specifically (ring finger keys raised, index columns angled inward), using low-profile switches to keep it compact and portable.
**Result:** A 36-key layout where each thumb covers three keys — space plus two "layer" keys — replacing dozens of rarely-reachable keys with layered combinations.
**Why it matters:** It's a concrete example of ergonomics-first hardware design overturning a century-old default that most people never question.
**Jargon:** *Columnar layout* — keys stacked in straight columns instead of offset rows. *Layer key* — a modifier that remaps other keys to a second function, like a hardware Shift. *Low-profile switch* — a shorter mechanical key switch for slimmer builds.

### 'Power of disorder' tames CF₄, a long-lived semiconductor greenhouse gas
**Problem:** CF₄, used in chip fabrication, is over 6,000x more potent than CO₂ and persists ~50,000 years. Catalysts meant to break it down corrode and clump under the heat, moisture, and fluorine involved, losing effectiveness fast.
**Approach:** KAIST researchers mixed five metals (Al, Zn, Ga, Ni, Co) evenly into one aluminate crystal structure, using "entropy stabilization" — structural disorder that resists clumping — to keep the catalyst intact under harsh conditions.
**Result:** In an 800°C, 150-hour accelerated test, conventional catalyst conversion fell from 93% to 48%; the new catalyst held at 98% to 92%, roughly 2.3x higher intrinsic activity overall.
**Why it matters:** A catalyst that keeps working under real fab conditions turns a lab result into something semiconductor plants could actually deploy to cut a very potent, very persistent greenhouse gas.
**Jargon:** *Entropy stabilization* — using atomic disorder to keep a mixed-metal structure from separating into simpler phases. *Aluminate* — a compound where metals bond around an aluminum-oxygen framework. *Dry etching* — using reactive gas (not liquid) to carve circuit patterns into a wafer. *Oxygen isotope tracing* — tagging oxygen atoms to track their path through a chemical reaction.
