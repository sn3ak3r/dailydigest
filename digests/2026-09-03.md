## Deep Digest: Genome Duplication Is a Radical Evolutionary Gamble

**Problem:** Why do organisms sometimes survive inheriting three or more full chromosome sets instead of two — an event usually lethal — and how does it drive evolution?

**Approach:** Researchers sequenced genomes of species with known duplications — yeast, New Zealand mud snails, goatsbeard plants, snowflake yeast — to track what happens to the extra genes over time.

**Result:** Mud snails lost 60% of duplicated genes within roughly a million years through rapid, non-random "genetic triage" — some genes kept, others dropped or repurposed — and the pattern repeats across generations.

**Why it matters:** Nearly all seed plants and many animal lineages trace major innovations, from spider silk glands to complex brains, back to ancient genome duplications.

**Glossary:** Polyploidy (extra chromosome sets), rediploidization (shrinking back toward normal), diploid (the standard two-set state), bioinformatics (computational gene analysis).

## Deep Digest: Doubling Thermal Printer Resolution by Wiggling

**Problem:** 1970s calculator thermal printers needed one costly heating element per pixel column, so higher resolution meant more elements and higher cost.

**Approach:** Sears 12 engineers made the print head physically wiggle side-to-side in sync with printing, letting one row of elements hit two dot positions per column.

**Result:** Effective resolution doubled with no extra hardware — at the cost of slightly wavy lines when timing drifted on long print runs.

**Why it matters:** A clean example of trading a small, tolerable defect for a big cost cut — a tradeoff still common in embedded hardware design.

**Glossary:** Thermal printer (heat-based printing), print head (the heating-element assembly), dot matrix (image built from a dot grid).
