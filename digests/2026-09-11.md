## Deep Digest: The Four-Color Theorem Gets a Rare New Proof

**Problem:** The four-color theorem (any map needs only four colors so no adjacent regions match) was proven in 1976 by exhaustive computer case-checking, but mathematicians never got a satisfying "why."

**Approach, in plain words:** Past proofs picked apart sparse regions of a map's graph one case at a time. The new team instead studied dense, triangle-packed "flat" regions, finding 8,202 reducible configurations that can all be verified simultaneously instead of sequentially.

**Result:** A new proof of the same theorem, but algorithmically far cheaper — roughly n² steps drops to n log n for a graph with n vertices.

**Why it matters:** Exposes new structure in planar graphs, opening paths toward coloring problems on other surfaces (like a torus) and other stubborn graph-theory questions.

**Jargon glossary:**
- *Planar graph* — a graph that can be drawn on a flat plane with no edges crossing.
- *Reducible configuration* — a local pattern provably reducible to a smaller, already-colorable case.
- *Unavoidable set* — a set of configurations guaranteed to appear in any planar graph.
- *Kempe chain* — a color-swapping trick used to resolve conflicts during recoloring.

## Deep Digest: Open Source Acoustic Drone Detection

**Problem:** Small quadcopter drones are fast and cheap, and RF/radar detectors miss them when they're hovering silently — a real gap for perimeter awareness.

**Approach, in plain words:** The VolAnti project listens instead of watching. Four microphones feed an ESP32-S3 chip, which runs a 2048-point FFT every 32ms to pull propeller-blade frequencies (70–2000 Hz) out of background noise. Several detection algorithms run in parallel, including one that skips adaptive noise filtering specifically so a stationary hovering drone doesn't get dismissed as background hum.

**Result:** An open-source, low-cost acoustic detector that catches both moving and silently hovering drones.

**Why it matters:** Gives hobbyists, researchers, and small operators a cheap, open alternative to expensive RF/radar drone-detection gear.

**Jargon glossary:**
- *FFT (Fast Fourier Transform)* — an algorithm that breaks an audio signal into its frequency components.
- *Adaptive noise floor* — a detection threshold that automatically rises with ambient noise.
- *Comb score* — a metric for how well a signal matches an expected pattern of harmonics.
- *I2S* — a digital audio interface standard used between mic and processor.
