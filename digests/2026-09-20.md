## Deep Digests

### Two-color light steers electrons through graphene's transient topological state
[phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-electrons-graphene-transient-topological-state.html)

Problem: Researchers wanted to control electrons inside a light-induced topological state in graphene, not just create the state.
Approach: They hit a graphene sheet with a strong 1,550nm circularly-polarized pulse to temporarily reshape its electronic structure (a Floquet state), then used a second, weaker 775nm pulse to steer electrons within that reshaped state, reading the result as current through gold electrodes.
Result: They saw current direction flip with light polarization, sideways electron deflection with no magnetic field, and current dominated by one electronic "valley" — all controllable within a single light cycle.
Why it matters: It's a step toward using light alone to engineer material properties on ultrafast timescales, useful for future optical switches and sensors.
Glossary: Floquet state - a temporary state induced by periodic driving light. Topological insulator - a material whose surface behavior is protected by its overall shape/symmetry. Valley - a distinct region of a material's electron-momentum map. Anomalous Hall effect - sideways electron deflection without a magnetic field.

### Coreboot Hikes the Bay Trail to DRAM Initialization
[hackaday.com](https://hackaday.com/2026/09/19/coreboot-hikes-the-bay-trail-to-dram-initialization/)

Problem: Coreboot needs to initialize DRAM before any RAM exists, which normally requires a manufacturer's closed-source binary blob — a dependency the project wants to remove.
Approach: Developers used the SerialICE debugger to capture the bus traffic of Intel Bay Trail's proprietary init code, then replayed and analyzed that behavior in the Unicorn CPU emulator to reverse-engineer the sequence without vendor documentation.
Result: Coreboot can now bring up DRAM on Bay Trail SoCs without that binary blob, on decade-old but still-used hardware.
Why it matters: It's a repeatable playbook for stripping proprietary blobs out of firmware, chipping away at vendor lock-in on aging but still-deployed silicon.
Glossary: Binary blob - closed-source firmware code with no available source. Coreboot - open-source BIOS/UEFI replacement. DRAM - the system's main working memory. SoC - system-on-chip, a processor with its support hardware integrated.
