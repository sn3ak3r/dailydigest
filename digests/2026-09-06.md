## Deep Digests

### Breaking through AlphaFold's limits to predict how proteins change shape

**Problem:** AlphaFold3 normally predicts one static protein shape, but many proteins work by flipping between multiple shapes — a blind spot for drug design.

**Approach:** Researchers added a "repulsive force" to AlphaFold3's diffusion model: each new prediction is penalized for landing too close to a shape it already generated, pushing it toward different ones.

**Result:** The tweaked model (AF3-ReD) found open, closed, and intermediate shapes of an ATP synthase subunit; stock AlphaFold3 only ever found the open one.

**Why it matters:** drugs often target one specific shape of a protein — seeing all the shapes makes design far more precise.

**Glossary:** diffusion model (AI that generates by refining noise into structure); conformation (a molecule's 3D shape); ATP synthase (enzyme that makes cellular energy); bias energy (a penalty term steering predictions).

### Running Generative AI on an RP2350

**Problem:** running image-generating AI usually needs a GPU and cloud servers — the opposite of cheap, private, offline hardware.

**Approach:** a hobbyist squeezed a diffusion transformer (the architecture family behind modern image generators) onto an RP2350, a sub-$1 microcontroller, by shrinking the model to 8-bit numbers so it fits in 4MB of flash.

**Result:** the chip generates recognizable 128×128 human faces in about 20 seconds each, shown over USB or VGA.

**Why it matters:** it shows current AI image techniques scale down to bare-metal hardware, opening the door to private, offline AI features in cheap embedded devices instead of cloud APIs.

**Glossary:** microcontroller (a tiny low-power computer chip); diffusion transformer (a neural network that generates images by denoising); quantization (shrinking number precision to save memory); VAE (variational auto-encoder, compresses images into a compact code).
