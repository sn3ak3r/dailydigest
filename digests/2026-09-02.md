## Deep Digests

### 1. Fire Ant turns Cisco routers into covert listening posts

**Problem.** Attackers want durable, undetected access to the network gear that sees everything passing through it, not just servers.

**Approach.** Incident responders at Sygnia found a hidden GRE tunnel on a Cisco IOS XR router. Investigation revealed the Chinese group "Fire Ant" had planted a backdoor (dubbed BridgeAgent) disguised as a monitoring agent, running as a root-level service, suppressing logs, and capturing traffic to upload to their own servers.

**Result.** The compromised routers became surveillance platforms, letting Fire Ant pivot into connected high-value networks while tampering with logs and timestamps to erase evidence.

**Why it matters.** Trusted infrastructure, not endpoints, is the new espionage target — it has a privileged view of everything around it.

**Jargon:** GRE tunnel — an encapsulated virtual link between two points. Root-level backdoor — malware running with full system privileges. IOC — indicator of compromise. Reverse shell — an attacker-controlled remote command session.

### 2. A cooler, faster path to forming the Moon

**Problem.** Standard giant-impact simulations model the Moon-forming collision as happening between molten bodies, ignoring how the material's temperature and strength affect the outcome.

**Approach.** Southwest Research Institute researchers reran the classic Theia-Earth impact simulation, this time adding temperature-dependent material strength for the colliding bodies.

**Result.** When both bodies were modeled as cooler and structurally stronger, an intact Moon assembled directly within about five hours — instead of slowly clumping together from a debris disk over a much longer period.

**Why it matters.** Since protoplanets cool as they age, the outcome ties the Moon's formation speed and composition to when the impact happened, and could help explain why Earth and the Moon share similar composition.

**Jargon:** Giant impact hypothesis — the leading theory that the Moon formed from a planet-sized collision with early Earth. Theia — the hypothesized Mars-sized impactor. Debris disk — orbiting material that gradually reaccretes. Protoplanet — an early-stage forming planet.
