## Deep Digest: AI Has Solved One of Math's $1 Million Millennium Prize Problems

**Problem:** Do the Navier-Stokes equations, describing fluid flow, ever "blow up" to infinite speed at a point? One of six unsolved Millennium Prize Problems.

**Approach, in plain words:** Two efforts converged. OpenAI ran 10,000 AI agents attacking it directly. Separately, mathematicians Buckmaster and Alpöge extended prior techniques, stacking "infinite layers" of well-behaved solutions into cascades that force a blow-up.

**Result:** A proof that singularities exist in the boundary-free 3D case, formally checked by the Lean proof assistant — the biggest AI-assisted math proof yet.

**Why it matters:** AI cracked a previously intractable open problem, and turbulence is confirmed stranger than assumed: flow can turn infinitely violent at a point.

**Jargon glossary:**
- *Navier-Stokes equations* — the standard equations for how fluids (water, air) move, accounting for viscosity.
- *Singularity (blow-up)* — a point where a quantity (here, fluid speed) becomes infinite.
- *Euler equations* — the frictionless (zero-viscosity) simplification of Navier-Stokes.
- *Lean* — a programming language/proof assistant that mechanically checks a proof's logic is valid.

## Deep Digest: ClearFake WebDAV Infection Chain

**Problem:** Cisco Talos traced a malware chain after spotting a DLL named "verification.google" launching from a WebDAV path at a Ukrainian government org.

**Approach, in plain words:** Victims on a compromised site see a fake Google CAPTCHA ("ClickFix") tricking them into pasting and running a command. That opens a WebDAV path and runs a disguised DLL via `rundll32.exe`. The overlay JavaScript comes from BNB Smart Chain contracts (EtherHiding), letting the attacker update payloads without hosting them.

**Result:** The chains deliver Amatera plus either a Zig-based crypto stealer with an EDR-killing driver, or a rogue NetSupport remote-access tool with Russian C2.

**Why it matters:** Shows how attackers blend social engineering, legitimate protocols, and blockchain hosting for stealthy, takedown-resistant delivery.

**Jargon glossary:**
- *WebDAV* — a protocol that lets Windows mount and run files from a remote server like a local drive.
- *ClickFix* — a social-engineering technique that tricks a user into manually running a malicious command.
- *EtherHiding* — storing/serving malicious code via blockchain smart contracts instead of a normal web server.
- *C2 (command-and-control)* — the attacker's server that issues instructions to installed malware.
