## Deep Digest: Exciton condensation precursors in a quantum material

**Problem.** Physicists debate whether spontaneously-forming electron-hole pairs (excitons) drive a known phase transition in the quantum material 1T-TiSe2, but the fleeting fluctuations that precede the transition are hard to catch directly.

**Approach.** Researchers fired ultrashort UV laser pulses at the material and timed how fast its electrons "reset" afterward, at temperatures above, at, and below the ~200K transition point.

**Result.** Right at the transition temperature, the electronic response snapped back unusually fast regardless of pulse strength — a fingerprint of excitons already pairing up before the ordered phase locks in.

**Why it matters.** Confirms excitons as a driver of this transition and gives a general technique for catching hidden fluctuations behind emergent phases in other quantum materials.

**Jargon glossary:**
- *Exciton* — a bound pair of an electron and the "hole" it left behind
- *Charge-density wave* — a periodic ripple in a material's electron density and atomic spacing
- *Ultrafast spectroscopy* — using extremely short laser pulses to watch electrons move
- *Phase transition* — a sudden change in a material's properties (like ice to water)

## Deep Digest: WiFiSense-Pi — detecting people through walls with WiFi

**Problem.** Motion sensors miss people who are sitting still, and dedicated presence sensors (radar, thermal) are specialized and costly.

**Approach.** An ESP32-S3 chip samples the ambient 2.4GHz WiFi signal 100 times a second; a Raspberry Pi crunches the tiny fluctuations a person's breathing causes in that signal, even through walls.

**Result.** A working, open-source presence detector with a live dashboard — it reliably tells whether a living body is in a room, though not where or how many.

**Why it matters.** Turns WiFi hardware everyone already owns into a whole-house occupancy sensor, cheaper and less intrusive than cameras or radar, for smart lighting, security, or eldercare monitoring.

**Jargon glossary:**
- *ESP32-S3* — a cheap microcontroller chip used in DIY electronics
- *WiFi sensing* — inferring motion/presence from how bodies disturb radio signals
- *Signal processing* — extracting useful patterns from noisy raw data
- *2.4GHz band* — the radio frequency WiFi and Bluetooth use
