## Deep digests

### Mathematicians Harness Randomness To Crack a 55-Year-Old Conjecture
Problem: Graham's 1971 conjecture asks whether any set of distinct nonzero residues mod a prime p can be ordered so all running (partial) sums differ. Approach: Start from a random ordering, set aside a few spare numbers, and patch any stretch summing to zero. Müyesser–Pokrovskiy handled near-full sets; Kravitz–Bedert handled tiny sets; a 2025 joint paper widened the large-set case. Result: Sauermann and Pham closed the medium-size gap in Feb 2026, completing the proof across four papers. Why it matters: it shows randomness plus small repairs can prove that ordered structures exist without constructing them. Glossary: partial sum (sum of the first k terms); residue mod p (clock-arithmetic number); probabilistic method (prove existence via random choice); combinatorics (math of arrangements).
