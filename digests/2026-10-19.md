## Deep digest: Physicists prove a long-suspected quantum speed limit for thermalization
**Problem:** Physicists saw hints of a universal speed limit on how fast quantum systems reach thermal equilibrium, but had no general proof.
**Approach:** Paolo Abiuso and colleagues treated thermalization as a process and used quantum information theory to compute the fastest it can happen, testing on a model of interacting particles.
**Result:** The minimum time is bounded by temperature and fundamental constants, independent of how hard the system is driven. Hotter means shorter allowed times; at very low temperature the gap between the two lowest energy states sets the limit. It ties to the Heisenberg limit.
**Why it matters:** A fundamental constraint for designing quantum technologies, including quantum computers.
**Glossary:** Thermal equilibrium (settled, uniform temperature); Qubit (quantum bit); Heisenberg limit (bound on measurement precision); Boltzmann constant (links temperature to energy).
