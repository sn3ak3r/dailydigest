## Deep digests

### New device measures curved mirrors' absolute shape to within 2 nanometers without touching them
Problem: Precision mirrors for EUV lithography and gravitational-wave detectors must be measured to nanometer accuracy, but touching them risks damaging coatings. Approach: AIST researchers measure the direction of light reflected from each point using a self-calibrating angle sensor, then reconstruct the full surface mathematically. Result: Noncontact measurement of a curved mirror's absolute shape to 2 nm accuracy. Why it matters: Manufacturers can verify and correct mirrors without contact, speeding up next-generation instruments. Glossary: EUV lithography (chip-making with extreme ultraviolet light); deflectometry (shape from reflected-light angles); metrology (science of measurement); wavefront control (shaping light behavior).

### AI structure prediction speeds discovery of 'molecular glues' to treat disease
Problem: Molecular glues destroy disease proteins instead of blocking them, but finding them is slow. Approach: Baylor researchers combined high-throughput proteomics, AI structure prediction and physics-based modeling in a tool called GluePlex, which predicts binding without prior experimental data. Result: They found and optimized NGT-201-18, which degrades VAV1, a protein tied to blood cancers and autoimmune disease, and suppressed T-cell activation in human cells. Why it matters: Predicting interactions before lab work could speed drugs for hard-to-treat immune and cancer diseases. Glossary: molecular glue (recruits a protein to cellular disposal); proteomics (measuring many proteins at once); cereblon (degradation-pathway protein); degron (surface degradation signal).
