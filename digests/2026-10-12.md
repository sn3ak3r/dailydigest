### Quantum 'birthmarks' hold lasting traces of a system's past
Problem: Isolated quantum systems may not forget their starting state the way chaotic classical ones do. Approach: Harvard-led theorists (Graf et al., Physical Review X, 2026) split the memory into a symmetry-set universal factor and a revival factor from early recurrences, tested numerically on a particle in a stadium-shaped box. Result: Long-run probabilities keep patterns tied to the initial state and early motion; scars strengthen it. A state that hasn't explored phase space by the Thouless time never will. Why it matters: Limits quantum ergodicity, which underlies thermalization. Glossary: ergodicity (long-term behavior independent of start); quantum scars (states on repeating classical paths); phase space (all positions and momenta); Thouless time (time to explore it).
