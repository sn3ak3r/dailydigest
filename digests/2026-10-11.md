### New stability rules distinguish quantum phases of matter that an older method wrongly groups together
Problem: Noisy quantum systems become mixed states with no Hamiltonian, so standard phase classification fails; the FDLC method wrongly lumps different phases together. Approach: Illinois researchers extended entanglement bootstrapping: three stability conditions pick fixed points, and phases are states approximately stable near them, labeled by topological invariants. Result: It separates a product state from the maximally dephased toric code, which FDLC conflates; deviations decay exponentially. Fracton phases aren't covered yet. Why it matters: Helps understand phases under noise, relevant to robust quantum hardware. Glossary: mixed state (noise-degraded state); fixed point (scale-invariant anchor); topological invariant (label unchanged by smooth deformation); FDLC (finite-depth local channel).
