### Quantum magic state cleanup cuts errors 40-fold in first successful hardware test
**Problem:** Universal quantum computing needs "magic states", but making them clean via distillation costs huge amounts of hardware.

**Approach:** Google Quantum AI ran the first hardware test of "magic state cultivation" on its Willow chip. A deliberately noisy state is spread over a small qubit grid; helper qubits check for errors without reading the data, and any run failing a check is discarded and restarted. A new "kickback tomography" verifies the tiny leftover error with far fewer measurements.

**Result:** Error fell 40-fold, to fidelity of about 99.99% or better; roughly 8% of attempts survived all checks. States were then moved into a surface-code-compatible layout as a proof of principle.

**Why it matters:** Magic states are a main resource bottleneck for fault-tolerant machines; cultivating them in place could cut the hardware needed.

**Glossary:** Magic state: special resource state enabling non-trivial gates. Distillation: filtering many noisy states into one clean one. Fidelity: closeness to the ideal state. Surface code: grid-based error-correcting code.
