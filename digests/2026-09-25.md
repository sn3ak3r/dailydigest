### Higher-Dimensional Black Holes Hide an Exact Symmetry in Their Ringing
[phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-higher-dimensional-black-holes-exact.html)

Problem: In gravity theories with more than four dimensions, do different kinds of black-hole vibrations ring at the same frequency, and does string theory change that?
Approach: Two mathematicians numerically computed the ringing frequencies of non-rotating black holes in 5-26 dimensions to 300 digits of precision, using a Chebyshev spectral method instead of standard approximations that fail on complex barrier shapes.
Result: In plain Einstein gravity, scalar-field vibrations and spacetime-twist vibrations ring at identical frequencies — a hidden symmetry. Adding a string-theory correction (a Gauss-Bonnet term) breaks that symmetry immediately.
Why it matters: It reveals hidden structure in Einstein's equations and shows where standard approximations fail, constraining theoretical models even though it's undetectable in real astronomical black holes.
Glossary: Quasinormal modes - a black hole's characteristic ringing frequencies. Gauss-Bonnet term - a string-theory curvature correction to Einstein's equations. Chebyshev spectral method - a high-precision numerical technique. Supersymmetric quantum mechanics - a framework where paired equations share identical spectra.

### AI-Guided Mutations Help Viruses Infect Bacteria Up to 1 Million Times More Effectively
[phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-ai-mutations-viruses-infect-bacteria.html)

Problem: Antibiotic resistance is outpacing new drug development, reviving interest in phage therapy — using viruses to kill bacteria — but natural phages often infect weakly or too broadly.
Approach: University of Wisconsin-Madison researchers trained an AI model on tens of thousands of lab-measured phage protein mutations to predict which changes improve infection efficiency and narrow host targeting.
Result: The model found mutations boosting bacterial-infection efficiency by up to a million-fold, while also identifying mutations that narrow a phage's targets to spare beneficial bacteria.
Why it matters: Computationally guided phage engineering could speed up alternatives to failing antibiotics, for infections like UTIs, while protecting the gut microbiome.
Glossary: Bacteriophage - a virus that infects bacteria. Phage therapy - treating infections with engineered viruses instead of antibiotics. Host specificity - a phage's ability to target only certain bacteria. High-throughput screening - lab testing of many genetic variants at once.
