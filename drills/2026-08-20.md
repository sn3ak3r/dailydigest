# Mini Crackme — 2026-08-20

Disassembly of `check` (Windows x64, single argument passed in `ecx`):

```asm
check:
    mov     eax, ecx
    xor     eax, 3Ch
    ror     eax, 3
    cmp     eax, 7
    jne     fail
    mov     eax, 1
    ret
fail:
    mov     eax, 0
    ret
```

`ror eax, 3` rotates all 32 bits of `eax` right by 3 positions — bits shifted off the low end wrap around into the high end.

Find the decimal value (0–255) passed in `ecx` that makes `check` return 1.

---

**Answer: 4**

- Work backwards from the compare: `eax` must equal `7` after the rotate, so before the rotate it must equal `rol(7, 3)` — rotating `0x00000007` left by 3 gives `0x00000038` (the `111` at the bottom shifts up to bits 5-3).
- That `0x38` is the value of `eax` right after the `xor`, so `ecx = 0x38 XOR 0x3C`. Since `0x38 = 00111000` and `0x3C = 00111100`, the XOR gives `00000100` = `0x04`.
- `ecx = 4` decimal. Both XOR and rotate are one-to-one on 32-bit values, so this is the unique byte that satisfies the check.
