# Decode-one-instruction drill — 2026-11-24

Target: Windows x86-64.

A function receives a 32-bit flags word in `ecx`. Right before a `jc` (jump if carry) you see:

```
0F BA E1 05
```

Decode it. If `ecx` holds `0x24`, is the jump taken? What C expression is being tested?

---

## Answer

`bt ecx, 5`: bit test. `0x24` = `100100b`, so bit 5 is 1. That sets **CF=1** and the `jc` **is taken**. The C is `if (flags & (1 << 5))`, i.e. `flags & 0x20`.

- `0F` is the two-byte escape you've seen before, and `BA` is the bit-test group that takes an imm8 bit number. ModRM `E1` = `11 100 001`: mod=11 means register-direct, reg=100 is the opcode extension **/4 = BT**, and rm=001 is ecx. The last byte, `05`, is the bit index.
- BT copies the chosen bit into the carry flag and leaves `ecx` unchanged. Unlike `test`, it doesn't use ZF, which is why the branch after it is `jc`/`jnc` rather than `je`/`jne`.
- The same group also writes the bit back after testing it: `/5 BTS` sets it, `/6 BTR` clears it and `/7 BTC` flips it. That's how `flags |= 0x20` can show up as `0F BA E9 05`.
