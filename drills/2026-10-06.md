# Decode-one-instruction drill — 2026-10-06

Target: Windows x86-64.

Decode this instruction (hex bytes):

```
48 C7 C0 FF FF FF FF
```

What is the assembly? What value ends up in `rax`, and why is the immediate only 4 bytes?

---

## Answer

`mov rax, -1` (rax = 0xFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF)

- `48` = REX.W (64-bit operand); `C7` = `MOV r/m, imm32` with ModRM `C0` = `11 000 000` → mod=11 register-direct, /0 opcode extension, rm=rax.
- The 32-bit immediate `FFFFFFFF` is sign-extended to 64 bits, so -1 becomes all ones; this is 7 bytes versus the 10-byte `mov rax, imm64` (`48 B8`).
- Contrast: `mov eax, -1` (`B8 FF FF FF FF`) also yields all-ones in rax? No — it gives 0x00000000FFFFFFFF, since the 32-bit write zero-extends.
