# Decode One Instruction (Windows x86-64)

A disassembler shows raw bytes at address `0x140001005` inside a function:

```
0x140001005: E8 D6 0F 00 00
```

Decode this single instruction: name the opcode, the addressing form of its operand, and compute the exact target address it transfers control to.

---

**Answer:** `call 0x140001FE0`

- `E8` is the opcode for **CALL rel32** (near, direct call) — a one-byte opcode followed by a 4-byte little-endian signed displacement, no ModRM byte at all since the target is instruction-pointer-relative rather than register/memory.
- The displacement bytes `D6 0F 00 00` read little-endian as `0x00000FD6` (4054); RIP-relative encodings are always computed from the address of the *next* instruction, not the CALL's own start.
- Next instruction address = `0x140001005 + 5` (opcode + 4 displacement bytes) = `0x14000100A`; adding the displacement gives `0x14000100A + 0xFD6 = 0x140001FE0`, and executing CALL also pushes the return address `0x14000100A` onto the stack before jumping.
