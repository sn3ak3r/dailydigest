# Decode-one-instruction drill — 2026-10-24

Target: Windows x86-64.

Decode this instruction, located at address `0x140001000`:

```
0F 8C 2A 01 00 00
```

What is the assembly (give the absolute target address)? Why is this two opcode bytes, and when would a compiler use a 2-byte form instead?

---

## Answer

`jl 0x140001130` (jump if less, signed).

- `0F` is the two-byte opcode escape; `0F 8C` = JL rel32 (near conditional jump; 0F 8x mirrors the short `7x` forms, so `7C` = JL rel8). Condition is SF != OF, i.e. signed less-than after a cmp.
- Displacement `2A 01 00 00` little-endian = 0x0000012A, signed, relative to the NEXT instruction: 0x140001000 + 6 = 0x140001006; + 0x12A = 0x140001130.
- Compilers emit the 2-byte `7C rel8` when the target is within -128..+127 bytes; otherwise they need this 6-byte rel32 form.
