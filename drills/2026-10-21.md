# Decode-one-instruction drill — 2026-10-21

Target: Windows x86-64.

Decode this instruction:

```
65 48 8B 04 25 60 00 00 00
```

What is the assembly? What does the `65` byte do, and why is there a SIB byte when no register is used for the address?

---

## Answer

`mov rax, qword ptr gs:[0x60]`

- `65` is the GS segment-override prefix; `48` is REX.W (64-bit operand); `8B` = MOV r64, r/m64. ModRM `04` = `00 000 100`: mod=00, reg=rax, rm=100 (SIB follows).
- SIB `25` = `00 100 101`: index=100 means "no index", base=101 with mod=00 means "no base, disp32 only". That is the only way to encode an absolute address in 64-bit mode (plain mod=00 rm=101 is RIP-relative). disp32 = `60 00 00 00` = 0x60.
- On Windows x64, GS points at the TEB, and TEB+0x60 holds the PEB pointer, so this loads the PEB address into rax (shellcode and malware use it to walk loaded modules).
