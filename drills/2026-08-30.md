# Daily Drill — 2026-08-30

**Format:** decode one instruction
**Target:** Windows x86-64

Decode this byte sequence into its full assembly instruction (mnemonic + operands):

```
8B 04 8D 00 10 00 00
```

Break down each piece: the opcode, the ModRM byte, and the SIB byte. Pay special attention to what `base=101` means in the SIB byte when `mod=00` — it isn't a normal base register.

---

## Answer

```
mov eax, [rcx*4 + 0x1000]
```

**Explanation:**
1. `8B` is `MOV r32, r/m32`. ModRM `04` = `mod=00, reg=000 (eax), rm=100` — `rm=100` means "no direct register, read a SIB byte next."
2. SIB `8D` = `scale=10b (×4), index=001b (rcx), base=101b`. With `mod=00` and `base=101`, this is the special no-base-register case: instead of using a base register, the encoding switches to a bare `disp32` (added to the scaled index), so there's no `[base + ...]` term at all.
3. The trailing 4 bytes `00 10 00 00` (little-endian) give `disp32 = 0x1000`. Effective address = `rcx*4 + 0x1000`, loaded into `eax`.
